# Research: writing a chronological, multi-tradition history of philosophy

Working guidelines for a planned book presenting the core thought of philosophers ordered through
time, across more than one philosophical tradition.

**This folder is research material, not the book.** It contains no draft chapters and no list of
philosophers. It exists so that when the writing starts, the structural decisions have already been
made deliberately and can be checked against something.

---

## How to use this

Documents `01` to `05` each end with a **checklist** to run against actual work. Beyond that they
differ, because the material does:

| File | Numbered rules | Failure modes | Checklist |
|---|---|---|---|
| `01` | P1-P10 principles | yes | yes |
| `02` | O1-O5 ordering rules | yes, F1-F8 | yes |
| `03` | C1-C3 charity limits | yes | yes |
| `04` | slots 1-7 of the chapter template | no | yes |
| `05` | the propagation table | no | yes |
| `06` | D1-D14 decisions | no | no, it has a Status table instead |

Cross-references use those labels: "O5 in `02`", "P9 in `01`", "slot 4", "see F5 below", "D3".
A bare `` `02` `` means the file `02-chronological-format.md`.

- **Starting the book?** Read `05` first, then `02`. The audience decision drives everything else,
  and the chronological structure is the hardest problem.
- **Drafting a chapter?** Work from the template in `04`, then run the checklists in `01` and `03`
  against the draft.
- **Reviewing a draft?** The checklists at the foot of `01` to `05` are the review. Start with `01`.
  `06` is not a review document; it is where project-level choices are recorded.
- **Making a project-level decision?** It is probably logged in `06`. Record the answer there.

## The short version

If only one page of this gets read:

1. **The audience decision comes first** and settles most of the rest. The proposal in `05` is the
   serious self-learner, with general-reader readability as a constraint.
2. **Chronology is a single line and the history of philosophy is not.** The recommended structure
   is braided: eras labelled by date range, each tradition running continuously inside an era, and a
   short synchronic section closing each era. `02`
3. **Give the reader the argument, not the position.** A survey that summarises views without the
   reasoning is what [Rorty 1984] calls doxography, and it is the standing risk of this format. `01`
4. **Keep exposition, interpretation and assessment distinguishable in every sentence.** Having a
   view is an asset; smuggling it into the philosopher's voice is the documented failure of
   [Russell 1945]. `03`
5. **Proportion is a promise.** A book that bills itself as covering Western *and* Eastern traditions,
   then gives everything outside the West an eighth of its pages **in total**, has told the reader
   something it did not mean to. `02`, and the case of [Grayling 2019] in [`sources.md`](sources.md)
6. **Resemblance is not influence.** Classify every connection as documented transmission, parallel
   development, or your own retrospective comparison, and phrase it accordingly. `02`
7. **Length is arithmetic.** Words divided by thinkers gives the chapter budget, and below roughly
   2,000 words an argument cannot be reconstructed. Breadth is bought with depth. `04`

## Attribution convention

Claims in this folder are marked so a future reader knows what kind of thing they are looking at:

- `[Author Year]` - traced to a specific source, which is listed in [`sources.md`](sources.md).
- `(synthesis)` - assembled from several sources, none of which states it in this form.
- `(judgement)` - a recommendation rather than a finding. Disagree with it freely.

No source is cited that has not been checked to exist, and [`sources.md`](sources.md) ends with an
honest list of what this bibliography does not cover. If you add a source, verify it and add it
there before citing it.

## Scope of this research

**Covered**: the principles of the genre, the chronological and multi-tradition format, faithful
exposition, structure and apparatus, audience and register.

**Not covered, by design**: any part of the book itself, the list of philosophers, and the
publishing or production pipeline. The open questions are logged in
[`06-decisions-to-make.md`](06-decisions-to-make.md); known gaps in the sources are logged at the
foot of [`sources.md`](sources.md).
